# COMMON — Five-Year Platform Vision and Scale Roadmap

> Working brand **COMMON**, provisional. Strategy horizon: 2027–2031, beginning after V1 launch. Ambitions are **scenarios, not forecasts or promises**.

## 1. North star
Build the world's most inclusive **skill and professional relationship graph**: anyone can demonstrate what they do; anyone can discover relevant people, ask for help, collaborate, learn, and find opportunities across local, regional and global contexts. A profession is not defined by a job title or degree.

The defensible asset is not merely a social feed. It is an **evidence-aware graph** linking people, skills, projects, organizations, work relationships, learning intents, and opportunities—without collapsing all evidence into an unaccountable trust score.

## 2. Permanent foundations
- People first, organizations second; identities portable and user-controlled.
- Inclusive discovery for informal trades, crafts, creative work, knowledge work, and emerging skills.
- Separate self-claims, mutual confirmations, and reviewed evidence.
- Relationships can be directed, mutual, multiple, contextual, time-bound, and disputed.
- Work-required skill endorsements; unconfirmed endorsements visibly unverified.
- Local/region/global discovery with privacy-preserving geography.
- Competency, experience, credentials, and professional stage remain distinct.
- Show evidence and explanations, not universal 'trust scores'.

## 3. Five-year roadmap
| Year | Strategic focus | Product/platform capabilities | Scale scenario* | Key proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Product-market fit in dense communities | Working V1; profiles, portfolios, graph, claims, scoped discovery, help, services, jobs, messaging; moderation | 10k–100k registered | Repeated successful help-to-contact and verified collaboration loops |
| 2 | Category and geographic expansion | Native mobile clients, stronger search, localization, creator/trade workflows, reference checks, richer business tools, API-first services | 100k–1M | Retention and marketplace liquidity across several skill categories |
| 3 | Intelligent skill and opportunity graph | Semantic matching with explanations, verified project histories, team assembly, learning pathways, skill gap insights, partner integrations | 1M–10M | Reliable matching lift over baseline and low dispute/abuse rates |
| 4 | Cross-border network and ecosystem | Multilingual/global discovery, credential partnerships, organization tooling, portable work evidence, integrations, scalable moderation | 10M–100M | Healthy regional ecosystems and sustainable unit economics |
| 5 | Global professional infrastructure | Developer platform, interoperable portable identity/evidence, enterprise sourcing, mature AI assistance, distributed operations | 100M–1B **stretch** | Large-scale trust, safety, utility and operating sustainability |

*Numbers are **illustrative ambition bands**, not predictions. A billion registered users in five years is an exceptionally unlikely stretch without extraordinary distribution, capital, partnerships, and localization. 'Registered', 'monthly active', and 'transacting' users must be reported separately.

## 4. What scale actually requires
### 10k → 100k: establish liquidity
Seed verified examples across both skilled trades and digital professions. Recruit community anchors, associations and training groups. Optimize for successful help matches, not vanity signups. Manual verification and moderation may be appropriate with audit trails.

### 100k → 1M: repeatable distribution
Expand city/category playbooks; partnerships with vocational schools, trade associations, universities and businesses. Introduce referrals, localized language and category-specific onboarding. Dedicated search and queues may become necessary.

### 1M → 10M: intelligent matching
Use semantic retrieval over opt-in public work and structured skills; deterministic filtering for location, availability and permissions. Human-auditable matching reasons; measured fairness across skill types and regions. No fabricated competency judgments.

### 10M → 100M: global operations
Multi-region infrastructure, data residency where required, abuse prevention, legal/compliance, multilingual support, professional credential providers, identity disputes and incident response. Partnerships and governance become as important as software.

### 100M → 1B: ecosystem, not one app
Portable professional profiles and attestations, partner APIs, developer ecosystem, institution integrations and reliable cross-border discovery. Avoid platform lock-in where possible. Success depends on trust and utility, not only feed engagement.

## 5. Product expansion tracks
**Identity and proof:** standardized claim schemas, review provenance, verifiable attestations where useful, skills evidence portability, expiry/revocation, appeal mechanisms.

**Relationships:** project-based collaboration graph, team histories, mentoring programs, directed learning links, optional private networks, business-to-person and business-to-business relationships; explicit consent for sensitive links.

**Opportunity network:** microtasks, gigs, jobs, apprenticeships, partnerships, team formation, project sourcing. Later, optional transaction infrastructure with dispute resolution and compliance.

**Learning:** mentorship marketplaces, expert sessions, learning circles, practical portfolios, pathways from Learning → Emerging → Practicing; do not equate paid work with inherent worth.

**Businesses:** shops, agencies, cooperatives, professional groups, teams, verified memberships, storefronts, workflow integrations, analytics.

**AI assistance:** natural-language needs → structured skills/constraints → explainable candidate suggestions; portfolio writing help; translation; fraud detection with review; consent-aware matching. Keep AI recommendations distinguishable from verified facts.

## 6. Technical evolution — scale by measured bottleneck
- **V1–100k:** modular monolith, PostgreSQL, object storage, background jobs, basic full-text search, CDN, metrics.
- **100k–1M:** read replicas, cache, dedicated search, async indexing, abuse/rate-control systems, partitioning where measured.
- **1M–10M:** graph-optimized read models or dedicated graph store **only if** relationship traversals justify it; event streaming, feature stores for matching, regional services.
- **10M–100M:** multi-region topology, regional data boundaries, resilience and disaster recovery, dedicated trust-and-safety infrastructure, privacy/compliance tooling.
- **100M–1B:** selective service decomposition, global routing, distributed search/graph infrastructure, high-availability and large-scale integrity operations.

**Avoid premature microservices or graph databases.** Instrument performance and costs, then evolve.

## 7. Sustainable business model hypotheses
- Free personal identity, portfolio, basic discovery and relationships.
- Paid business pages and advanced service tooling.
- Recruiter and organization sourcing subscriptions with strong privacy controls.
- Optional paid verification/review processing **without buying a positive badge**; ensure free pathways to basic participation.
- Optional transaction fees on future facilitated services (only after trust/safety and legal readiness).
- APIs/institutional partnerships later.

Validate willingness to pay and avoid turning visibility into pay-to-win rankings.

## 8. North-star metrics and safeguards
**North-star candidate:** monthly **meaningful outcomes** (help requests resulting in contact/engagement, mutually confirmed collaborations, completed mentoring sessions, or fulfilled opportunities), with strict event definitions and fraud filtering.

Supporting: monthly active people, search success, time to useful match, help-request fill rate, confirmed relationships, profile completeness, repeat collaboration, category coverage, creator/provider retention, complaint rate, dispute resolution time, false verification rate, privacy incidents, gross margin and infrastructure cost per active user.

Set regional/category fairness checks. Never use sheer connections, endorsements or badges as the sole ranking metric.

## 9. Risk register and countermeasures
| Risk | Countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Cold-start and empty search results | Focus on dense local/category communities, seed legitimate opt-in providers |
| Fake work claims / endorsement rings | Provenance, disputes, abuse heuristics, manual review, confirmed/unconfirmed separation |
| Harassment / doxxing via public claims | Visibility controls, claim challenges, rapid takedown, private sensitive ties |
| Bias toward degrees, elite jobs or big cities | Evidence-inclusive taxonomy, category-aware criteria, localized matching audits |
| Verification costs grow too quickly | Tiered review, partners, expiry policy, automation with human appeals |
| Broad feature sprawl | Gate expansions on core outcome and retention evidence |
| Regulatory and cross-border complexity | Region-by-region rollout, counsel review, data minimization |
| AI hallucinated skills or reputation | Retrieval grounded in user-approved evidence, explicit uncertainty, auditability |
| Monetization damages trust | Separate sponsored visibility from verified proof; transparent policies |

## 10. Stage gates / stop-go decisions
**Before expanding beyond first communities:** show repeat usage and real completed help/collaboration outcomes.

**Before automated verification:** measure false positives/negatives against human-reviewed reference sets, create appeals and revoke pathways.

**Before cross-border transactions:** assess payments, consumer protection, tax, identity, safeguarding and dispute obligations.

**Before each 10x scale investment:** show retention, liquidity, unit economics, safety operations and technical need; do not scale merely to hit a signup headline.

## 11. Agent handoff: how to use this vision
1. V1 is already built — see `docs/` (start at `docs/product-overview.md`) for what
   shipped and `docs/decisions.md` for open questions. This vision is **not**
   authorization to ship all five years of features now; treat it as direction for
   what V1's architecture should stay extensible toward, not a backlog to start on.
2. Keep domain models extensible (skill taxonomy, evidence provenance, relationship types, visibility, localized labels), but build only needed V1 functionality.
3. Keep business logic behind typed service interfaces so native apps, integrations and future search engines can reuse it.
4. Write Architecture Decision Records for major technology choices and migration triggers.
5. Flag product-policy decisions requiring human review instead of silently inventing verification or reputation rules.

## 12. Remaining strategic questions
- Initial beachhead market, country/city and professional categories?
- Strongest repeat-use loop: discover provider, find opportunity, or learn/collaborate?
- What evidence can be verified cost-effectively for informal trades?
- Which regional regulations and minors' protections apply?
- How do we distinguish completed work from claimed work at scale?
- How much of the social feed improves professional outcomes versus distracts from them?
